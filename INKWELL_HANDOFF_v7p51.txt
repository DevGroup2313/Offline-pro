WHY 7.51
--------
The writer asked for the work after 7.50 to reach the DevGroup2313

the card; the message at the foot of the page was ink-black on cream).

WHAT CHANGED
------------
1. The comma after an opening phrase or clause (src/lib/learner_core.js,
   the comma family; tools/grammar/README.md, "After 7.50", first part).
   A prepositional phrase of three words or more before a pronoun subject,
   and an opening clause or phrase before a determiner and its noun, where
   the main clause follows at once with its verb; nothing where a second
   subject and verb come soon after ("By the time I arrived the party was
   over"), where the determiner's word can only be a verb or a pronoun, or
   where an opener is followed by a preposition, "when", "which" or "as".
   Twenty-two more openers. W&I tuning half F0.5 0.284 to 0.291, report
   half 0.257 to 0.261, CoNLL-2014 0.270 to 0.271.
2. Precision on published prose (the same README, second part). Every
   change on the two correct sets was read by reason; seven misfiring
   shapes guarded, each with a test: a collective noun with a plain verb
   gets the note without a fix (British usage); a day or a year before a
   capitalised word is a title or an event; a word with an inner capital
   is a name; after a number an -s word that may be a verb heads the
   compound; "this ends up" is a verb and its particle; "as they pleased"
   lacks no verb; "on the night" stays. Correct prose changed 4.8% to
   4.5% (tuning) and 5.1% to 4.8% (report); precision up on every learner
   set, recall kept. The article rule allows an adverb before the
   adjective ("is a very beautiful place").
3. The rule after a rewrite (inkRwToast in src/inkwell.html): when a
   rewrite is used, the line at the foot of the page carries the first
   change kept that a rule explains, with its reason, for a breath (The
   rule after a fix, My way of working); the undo chip stays. The "well
   known" recast has its own reason (rewrite_core.js WHYS.held). The rule
   in a breath (inkRuleLine) takes a lesson's first sentence, and the
   second when the first is only a short question, within two rows.
4. The look. The message at the foot of the page (#toast) wears the
   cards' face: cream with a brass edge by day, the night cards' midnight
   by night, the receipt word (Accepted, Applied) in brass. The practice
   card's Done button sits at the right at its own width, and Check beside
   the answer box (the card's row had stretched each button across it).
