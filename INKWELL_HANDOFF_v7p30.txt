============================

Fire tablet, iPad and iPhone. Read INKWELL_HANDOFF_v7p29.txt for everything

--------------------------

  src/inkwell.html         the desk (the main page)

CHANGES IN 7.30 (STAGE 1)
=========================
1. The Mini model (grammar) did not load on the Fire tablet.
   The Fire app counts as a low-power device, so it tried the "bundled" model
   first. That model exists only in the desktop packages (webmodels/), so the
   attempt failed three times, AND it left the model runtime switched to
   "look in webmodels/ first" for every later model. Fixed: the page now
   checks whether the bundled model is present before trying it, and always
   restores the runtime's settings after a bundled attempt.
   Verified in Chromium posing as the Fire app, at the Fire app's own address:
   before, every later download first asked webmodels/ for its files; after,
   only one presence check goes there, and the model installs from the
   internet (LaMini-Flan-T5-248M in the passing run).

2. Scriba did not work inside Tabula.
   Two causes: Tabula's own Scriba button used the browser's speech engine,
   which the Fire and iPhone/iPad apps do not have, and Tabula's frame was not
   allowed the microphone. Now Tabula's Scriba button opens Inkwell's own
   Scriba room (local server, on-device Whisper, or the browser engine,
   whichever the device has); its button reads "Insert into Tabula" and puts
   the words exactly where the cursor was in Tabula.
   Verified: "Before after." with the cursor between the words became
   "Before middle words after."; opened from the desk afterwards, Scriba is
   the ordinary Scriba again ("Send to Tabula"). No page errors.

3. Scriba's Dictate button seemed to do nothing.
   "Dictate" was only a tab that switched to the dictation view, which was
   already showing. Now, on the dictation view, Dictate starts and stops
   dictation exactly as Record does. Also, once on-device Whisper has loaded,
   Scriba now says it is ready, instead of leaving the download count
   ("Fetching on-device Whisper ... 42 MB") on screen.
   Verified with a simulated microphone: Record, Stop, and the view switch.

4. The paper stayed small when the Documents or Suggestions panel was folded.
   Below 1100 px wide (every phone and tablet) a flex size outranked the
   folded width, so folded panels kept their full columns; on wide screens
   the paper was also capped at 820 px. Now a folded panel is a slim rail and
   the paper takes all the freed width. On phones (under 700 px) both panels
   start folded, and an opened panel slides over the paper instead of
   squeezing it.
   Measured, paper width with both panels folded, before -> after:
     iPhone 393 px wide       99 -> 346 px
     iPad 1024 px            616 -> 914 px
     Fire, portrait 1200     820 -> 1072 px
     Fire, landscape 1920    820 -> 1792 px
     Laptop 1440             820 -> 1312 px

5. The "Show footer" tab showed through Tabula, Scriba and Fontes.
   Now it shows only on the desk itself. Verified for each room, open and
   closed again.

6. Bands at the top and bottom of the apps.
   iPhone and iPad: the grey bands were the app's own background showing in
   the status-bar and home-indicator areas. Fire: the navy bands were the
   status and navigation bars, painted navy by the app's theme. Now the page
   reports the colours at its own top and bottom edges (day, night, and when
   a room opens) and the app paints those areas to match, with dark or light
   status-bar icons to suit. The shared script is
   Inkwell-Android/app/src/main/assets/ink-host/edge-colours.js.
   Verified: the Android app's real Java code assembles the page, and in
   Chromium the page calls setEdges with its colours, and new ones in night
   mode. The iOS side compiles on GitHub's Mac.

Versions: the Fire app is 7.30 (version code 73000 + build number, so it
installs over every 7.29 build and keeps the documents); the iPhone/iPad app
and the web app are 7.30.

7.30, SECOND ROUND (after your report that every device still said 7.29)
=======================================================================
Why everything said 7.29: the visible version labels were never bumped. The
page's BUILD label and the desktop launcher's LAUNCHER_BUILD still read 7.29,
so 7.30 installs looked like 7.29. Both now say 7.30, and the masthead shows
the version beside the date on every device:  I · OCTOBRIS · MMXXVI · v7.30
If a device does not show v7.30 there, it is not running 7.30.

Night mode in a floating Tabula window reverted to day within 1 to 4 s.
  Tabula's own theme check read Carpe Diem's mark (data-theme="night" on the
  body); Inkwell marks night on its root (data-theme="dark"). It now reads
  both. Tested: Tabula, Scriba and Fontes, full-screen and floating, opened in
  day and in night, toggled from the desk, the room's moon and the window's
  moon, checked at 0.5 s and 6 s: 96 of 96 correct, run twice.

Rewrite (all ten styles) and the Draft button did nothing (already in 7.29):
  they called rewrite() and compose(), which no longer existed. Restored: the
  offline rules engine answers at once (concise, professional, scholarly,
  casual, confident, shorter); the server or the Pro model adds its version;
  summary, expand, paraphrase and natural need a writing model and say so.
  Draft asks what to write and inserts at the cursor. Nothing changes the
  page until "Use this". Preset and custom rewrites of the whole page now
  replace the page instead of inserting a copy at the cursor.

Scriba's Copy said "Copied" before the clipboard answered; when the clipboard
  refused (the app windows can), nothing was copied. It now waits, falls back
  to a second copy method, and says so honestly if both are refused.

Every control pressed in a test browser, with errors recorded:
  desk 111 controls, Tabula 395, Scriba 10, Fontes 15: no errors left. (The
  only control that closes a room is Tabula's New window, by design.)

7.30, THIRD ROUND: ONE ECOSYSTEM FOR DICTATION
=============================================
Scriba inside Tabula now writes straight onto Tabula's page; the Scriba room
does not open. Scriba's own engine (the local server first, then on-device
Whisper, then the device's own speech engine) runs in a hidden frame in
"relay" mode and hands every finished phrase to the page that asked. Scriba's
own page works exactly as before, and its saved pad is never touched by
dictation into Tabula or the desk.
  In Tabula: press the microphone. It glows while listening, and a small note
    on the page says what Scriba is doing. Press it again to stop.
  On the desk: Insert, "Dictate here": the same, at the desk's caret.
  On the desk: Insert, "Scriba's pad at the caret": brings in what was
    dictated earlier in Scriba's own page.
  In Scriba's room: "Into the document" (beside "Send to Tabula") puts the pad
    into the open Inkwell document at the caret.
  After Stop, phrases already spoken are still written (the model may have
    been loading, with speech queued); once Scriba reports its queue empty,
    nothing more is accepted, and never more than three minutes after Stop.
  Tested in a browser with a simulated microphone and the real on-device
  Whisper: text landed at the cursor in Tabula and on the desk; the Scriba
  room never opened; the pad was untouched; a stray phrase after Scriba
  reported done was rejected. Regression: boot on four devices, Rewrite and
  Draft, and the 96-check night-mode matrix, all passing.

7.31: THE FIRE TABLET AND YOUR LAPTOP'S MODEL
============================================
Look for "v7.31" beside the date in the masthead. If it is not there, the
device is not running 7.31.

1. Your laptop's model, from the Fire tablet and the iPad (Stage 2).
   Before 7.31 the tablet was told "the server is the better path", but
   nothing on the laptop listened to the home network, and the tablet app's
   window may not contact a laptop by itself (Android and iOS block an app
   page from reaching plain-http addresses on the home network). Now:
   - On the laptop (Mac or Windows, 7.31 installed): Engine, under "Your
     laptop's model", press "Share with my tablets on this Wi-Fi". It shows
     an address (for example 192.168.1.20:8318) and a 6-digit code. The
     first time, Windows or macOS may ask whether to allow Inkwell on the
     network: choose Allow (on Windows, "Private networks").
   - On the tablet, same Wi-Fi: Engine, "Your laptop's model": type the
     address and the code, press "Connect to my laptop". The engine pill
     changes to the laptop's model.
   - The laptop listens on a second port (8318) that answers ONLY the
     writing tools (corrections, rewrites, drafting, review, Scriba's
     transcription), ONLY with the code. Your library, the model shelf and
     the settings stay reachable only from the laptop itself. "Stop
     sharing" closes the port; "New code" unpairs every tablet. Nothing is
     reachable from the network until you switch sharing on.
   - The Fire and iPhone/iPad apps make the connection themselves (natively)
     and hand the answer to the page.
   Tested here with the real laptop server and two browser windows, one as
   the laptop and one as the Fire app (with every direct request from the
   Fire page blocked, so only the app's own path could work): wrong code
   refused and reported as a wrong code; right code connected; a correction
   and a rewrite came back from the laptop; after "Stop sharing" the tablet
   showed it disconnected. From the network, the library, model upload and
   the sharing switch answer "not found".

2. "Diagnose this device" (Engine panel) for the Mini model on the Fire.
   The Mini model loads in every test here, including Chromium posing as
   the Fire app with its processor slowed six times, so the cause on your
   tablet cannot be seen from here. The report checks, step by step and
   timed: the app's web engine and its version, WebAssembly, storage, the
   model cache, the runtime files inside the app, starting the runtime,
   reaching huggingface.co (where the models come from) and a 2 MB
   download, then the install log word for word. "Copy the report" or
   "Save the report", and send it: the first FAIL line names the cause.
   If the Chrome version it reports is below 119, update "Android System
   WebView" from the Amazon Appstore, if your Fire offers it (I am not
   certain every Fire model does).
   The Fire app now also recognises itself as a Fire tablet (its window
   names the model, KFSNWI, rather than Silk).

3. "Between your devices": on a tablet connected to the laptop, the Push
   and Pull buttons are replaced by an honest note. Over the home Wi-Fi the
   laptop shares its model only, never your documents, until Stage 3. Use
   File, Export library and File, Import library meanwhile.

Versions: the Fire app 7.31 (version code 73100 + build number), the
iPhone/iPad app and the web app 7.31, the Mac and Windows launcher 7.31
with server.py 2.12. The launcher's protections for your documents are
unchanged from 7.29; only the server gained the sharing port.
